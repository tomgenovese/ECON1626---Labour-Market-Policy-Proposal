# Use of AI Reflection

I used **Claude Opus 5** initially to test how well AI could handle the assignment in one attempt. I “one-shot” the task to see what it would produce, but the result was too broad and tried to address too many labour-market problems and policy responses at once. I therefore rejected that overall direction.

I then worked iteratively with **ChatGPT using GPT-5.6 Sol** to narrow the proposal. A major change was moving from a broad discussion of AI and employment to a specific DEWR policy proposal focused on protecting entry-level pathways in occupations with high generative-AI exposure. GPT-5.6 Sol helped me refine the problem definition, make the three policy options more specific, and improve their mechanisms, target groups and costings.

I found and checked the underlying sources myself, particularly DEWR and Jobs and Skills Australia material, rather than relying on AI-generated summaries. I also used GPT-5.6 Sol for editing and to convert the final proposal into clean Markdown for GitHub.

The main limitation I noticed was that AI can produce confident but overly broad or imprecise analysis, so I had to verify claims against primary sources and substantially edit the outputs before using them.
