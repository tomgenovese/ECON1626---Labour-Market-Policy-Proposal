Proposal.MD

The Deputy Secretary, Employment and Workplace Relations Policy, at the Department of Employment and Workplace Relations (DEWR), the federal department. The Minister is Amanda Rishworth.

DEWR published a report called AI and Employment in Australia on 8 July 2026, written by its Office of the Chief Economist. It was commissioned to build a framework for monitoring AI's effect on the job market, and the government said it will keep running that monitoring.

So the process is: that monitoring framework, and what the department does next with it. 
This brief is addressed to the Deputy Secretary, Employment and Workplace Relations Policy, Department of Employment and Workplace Relations, and responds to the AI and Employment in Australia report (Office of the Chief Economist, July 2026) and the ongoing labour-market monitoring framework it establishes.




The problem
AI isn't getting people fired. It's stopping people getting hired.

If a job is the kind AI is good at — admin, clerical, data entry — companies aren't sacking anyone. They just don't replace people who leave, and they post fewer new roles. So the job count looks fine, but the jobs are quietly disappearing from the bottom.

That's the thing your brief points out: nobody's watching for this, because governments watch unemployment numbers, and unemployment looks totally normal.


The impact

Jobs AI can do grew 5.6% over the last few years. Jobs AI can't do grew 9.5%. Same economy, same period. That gap is the whole story.

Who it hits: office and admin workers, which is mostly women. And graduates, because entry-level office jobs are exactly the ones AI does well — so the first rung of the career ladder is being sawn off.



Three things the government could do

Free retraining, but you get it before you lose your job. Right now you usually have to be unemployed to qualify for help. Flip it: if you work in a job AI is coming for, you get free TAFE or short courses while you're still employed. Costs a moderate amount. Fair, but honestly the evidence that adult retraining works isn't great — you should say so.

Set an alarm. The government already collects this data, it just writes reports with it. Instead, agree in advance: "if the gap between AI-exposed and non-exposed jobs hits X, support kicks in automatically." Almost free, because the data already exists. But on its own it does nothing — it just tells you when to act.

Pay employers to hire juniors. A subsidy for taking on entry-level staff in affected industries, as long as there's real training attached. Helps graduates most. Very expensive, and you often end up paying companies to do things they'd have done anyway.
